ENRICHED HERPETOLOGICAL & CLINICAL GUIDE TO INDIAN SNAKE SPECIES

[SPECIES: ahaetulla prasina]
Common Name: Asian Vine Snake / Oriental Whip Snake
Local Names: Hindi: हरहरा (Harhara); Gujarati: લીલવણ (Lilvan)
Toxicity & Category: MILDLY VENOMOUS (Rear-fanged; Harmless to humans)
Venom Type: Mild neurotoxic (only affects small lizards/frogs)
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: Not required (no systemic effect on humans)
Average Size: 1.0 to 1.5 meters (extremely thin, thread-like body)
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Bright leaf-green or light brown body, pointed long snout, horizontal keyhole-shaped pupils.
Active Hours & Habits: Diurnal and Arboreal (lives in shrubs, trees, and gardens).
Geographic Distribution in India: Western Ghats, Northeast India, West Bengal, and Andaman Islands.
Diet & Ecological Role: Geckos, tree frogs, small lizards, and small birds.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Opens mouth wide showing black/white inter-scale markings to threat. Rarely bites; bite causes minor local itching or mild swelling at most.

[SPECIES: amphiesma stolatum]
Common Name: Buff Striped Keelback
Local Names: Hindi: सीता लट (Sita Lat); Gujarati: દંડિયા સાપ (Dandiya)
Toxicity & Category: NON-VENOMOUS (Completely harmless)
Venom Type: None
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: Not Applicable
Average Size: 0.4 to 0.8 meters (small, gentle snake)
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Strongly keeled scales; brownish body with two distinct bright yellow or buff longitudinal stripes running along the spine.
Active Hours & Habits: Diurnal; common around water bodies, paddy fields, and wet gardens.
Geographic Distribution in India: Pan-India.
Diet & Ecological Role: Frogs, toads, and small fish.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Exceptionally docile. Never attempts to bite humans even when handled; flattens body against the ground when scared.

[SPECIES: bungarus caeruleus]
Common Name: Common Krait
Local Names: Hindi: करैत (Karait), डोमिया (Domiya); Gujarati: કળતરા (Kalatra)
Toxicity & Category: HIGHLY VENOMOUS (Big Four Species)
Venom Type: Pre-synaptic Neurotoxic (Inactivates nerve terminals)
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: FULLY NEUTRALIZED by standard Indian Polyvalent ASV
Average Size: 0.9 to 1.2 meters (up to 1.7 meters)
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Glossy, jet-black or steel-blue body with narrow paired white cross-bands; distinctive enlarged hexagonal dorsal scales along the spine.
Active Hours & Habits: Strictly Nocturnal; remains hidden under debris or in burrows during daylight.
Geographic Distribution in India: Pan-India (plains, open forests, agricultural fields, often enters huts/rural homes at night during monsoon).
Diet & Ecological Role: Ophiophagous (eats other snakes, including cobras and rat snakes), small mammals, and lizards.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Sluggish and docile during the day, coiling with head tucked under body. Highly active at night; bites sleeping humans on the floor without audible warning or loud hissing.
Key Clinical Bite Symptoms: Bite wound causes minimal or zero local pain or swelling. Patients often wake up in the morning with severe abdominal cramps, joint pain, drooping eyelids, and progressive respiratory failure.
Common Look-alikes: Non-venomous Common Wolf Snake (Lycodon aulicus) and Bridal Snake.

[SPECIES: bungarus fasciatus]
Common Name: Banded Krait
Local Names: Hindi: अहिराज (Ahiraj); Gujarati: પીળો કરૈત (Pilo Karait)
Toxicity & Category: HIGHLY VENOMOUS
Venom Type: Neurotoxic
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: PARTIALLY NEUTRALIZED by standard Indian Polyvalent ASV
Average Size: 1.5 to 2.1 meters
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Triangular body cross-section with prominent dorsal ridge; alternating bold black and bright yellow/butter-colored bands encircling the body; blunt tail tip.
Active Hours & Habits: Strictly Nocturnal.
Geographic Distribution in India: Northeast India, West Bengal, Odisha, Bihar, and Jharkhand.
Diet & Ecological Role: Strict ophiophage (hunts other snakes, including vipers and rat snakes).
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Extremely timid during the day; hides its head under its coils. Rarely bites humans unless deliberately provoked or trodden upon at night.

[SPECIES: chrysopelea ornata]
Common Name: Ornate Flying Snake
Local Names: Hindi: उड़ने वाला सांप; Gujarati: ઉડતો સાપ (Udto Sap)
Toxicity & Category: MILDLY VENOMOUS (Rear-fanged; Harmless to humans)
Venom Type: Mild Cytotoxic (paralyzes small lizards)
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: Not required
Average Size: 0.9 to 1.2 meters
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Slender black body with yellow-green crossbars and red flower-like dorsal spots. Flattens its ribs into a concave shape to glide through the air between tree branches.
Active Hours & Habits: Diurnal and Arboreal.
Geographic Distribution in India: Western Ghats, Bihar, West Bengal, Northeast India.
Diet & Ecological Role: Tree lizards, geckos, bats, bird eggs.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Agile and fast; flees into tree canopy when threatened.

[SPECIES: daboia russelii]
Common Name: Russell's Viper
Local Names: Hindi: घोणस (Daboia), कोडिया (Kodiya); Gujarati: ચિતળ (Chital), ખડચિતળો (Khadchitalo)
Toxicity & Category: HIGHLY VENOMOUS (Big Four Species)
Venom Type: Potent Haemotoxic, Cytotoxic, and Myotoxic
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: FULLY NEUTRALIZED by standard Indian Polyvalent ASV
Average Size: 1.2 to 1.5 meters (robust, thick-bodied)
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Deep triangular head, distinct neck, strongly keeled dorsal scales. Pattern consists of three longitudinal series of dark brown oval blotches outlined in white/black like chain links.
Active Hours & Habits: Nocturnal and Crepuscular; hides in grassy thickets during heat of day.
Geographic Distribution in India: Widespread across India (open fields, farmlands, brushwood, rocky areas; absent in dense rainforests and extreme high altitudes).
Diet & Ecological Role: Major rodent predator (rats, bandicoots, mice).
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Highly defensive when disturbed. Coils into an S-shape and produces a booming, high-pitched hiss resembling a pressure cooker valve. Strikes with explosive speed and deep fang penetration.
Key Clinical Bite Symptoms: Instant severe local pain, rapid swelling, blistering, local necrosis, persistent bleeding from bite site, systemic bleeding (gums, nose, stomach), acute kidney injury, and low blood pressure.

[SPECIES: dendrelaphis pictus]
Common Name: Painted Bronzeback Tree Snake
Local Names: Hindi: कांस्य अजगर प्रकार; Gujarati: તાંબડી સાપ (Tambdi Sap)
Toxicity & Category: NON-VENOMOUS (Harmless)
Venom Type: None
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: Not Applicable
Average Size: 0.9 to 1.2 meters
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Bronze-brown dorsal color, bright yellow/white lateral stripe bounded by a dark line along the lower body. Wide blue/turquoise skin visible between scales when inflated.
Active Hours & Habits: Diurnal and Arboreal; active jumper among low trees and bushes.
Geographic Distribution in India: Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, West Bengal, Northeast India.
Diet & Ecological Role: Tree frogs, lizards.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Non-aggressive; relies on lightning speed to evade predators.

[SPECIES: naja naja]
Common Name: Indian Cobra / Spectacled Cobra
Local Names: Hindi: नाग (Naag), गेहुअन (Gehuan); Gujarati: નાગ (Naag)
Toxicity & Category: HIGHLY VENOMOUS (Big Four Species)
Venom Type: Post-synaptic Neurotoxic & Cardiotoxic
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: FULLY NEUTRALIZED by standard Indian Polyvalent ASV
Average Size: 1.0 to 1.5 meters (up to 2.2 meters)
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Smooth dorsal scales, round pupils, iconic hood with a spectacle or monocle mark on the posterior side when raised.
Active Hours & Habits: Diurnal and Crepuscular; active during early morning and dusk.
Geographic Distribution in India: Pan-India (found in all states across plains, agricultural fields, scrub forests, and urban areas).
Diet & Ecological Role: Primarily feeds on rats, mice, frogs, toads, birds, and eggs. Attracted to human dwellings due to rodent infestations.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Prefers to escape. When cornered, rears up its front body, expands its hood, and emits a loud, rhythmic hiss. Bites if stepped on or grabbed.
Key Clinical Bite Symptoms: Rapid onset of Ptosis (drooping eyelids), facial palsy, difficulty swallowing (Dysphagia), slurred speech, respiratory paralysis within 2 to 6 hours if untreated.
Common Look-alikes: Oriental Rat Snake (Ptyas mucosa) when the cobra's hood is folded.

[SPECIES: ophiophagus hannah]
Common Name: King Cobra
Local Names: Hindi: राजनाग (Rajnag); Gujarati: કિંગ કોબ્રા (King Cobra)
Toxicity & Category: HIGHLY VENOMOUS
Venom Type: Neurotoxic & Cytotoxic (Injects massive venom volume)
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: POORLY NEUTRALIZED by standard Big Four Indian Polyvalent ASV (Requires species-specific monovalent ASV)
Average Size: 3.0 to 4.5 meters (World's longest venomous snake)
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Large occipital scales on head, chevron/v-shaped light crossbands along body, narrower hood than Indian Cobra.
Active Hours & Habits: Diurnal; active hunter in forest canopies and river banks.
Geographic Distribution in India: Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, Odisha, West Bengal, Northeast India, and Andaman Islands.
Diet & Ecological Role: Strict ophiophage (eats almost exclusively other snakes, including vipers, cobras, and rat snakes).
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Intelligent and alert. Prefers to avoid humans. When confronted, rears up to one-third of its length, looks humans in the eye, and produces a deep growl-like hiss.
Key Clinical Bite Symptoms: Rapid systemic neurotoxicity, severe local tissue destruction, respiratory arrest within 30 minutes to 2 hours due to high venom yield.

[SPECIES: psammodynastes pulverulentus]
Common Name: Mock Viper
Local Names: Hindi: नकली वाइपर; Gujarati: મોક વાઇપર
Toxicity & Category: MILDLY VENOMOUS (Rear-fanged; Harmless to humans)
Venom Type: Mild
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: Not required
Average Size: 0.3 to 0.6 meters (small size)
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Broad triangular head mimicking a true pit viper, vertical elliptical pupils, dark brown or grey mottled body. Lacks heat-sensing facial pits.
Active Hours & Habits: Nocturnal and Crepuscular; lives in forest leaf litter and shrubs.
Geographic Distribution in India: Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, Northeast India.
Diet & Ecological Role: Geckos, skinks, small frogs.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Strikes aggressively when cornered to mimic a viper, but its bite poses no danger to humans.

[SPECIES: ptyas korros]
Common Name: Indochinese Rat Snake
Local Names: Hindi: धामिन प्रजाति; Gujarati: ધામણ જાતિ
Toxicity & Category: NON-VENOMOUS (Harmless to humans)
Venom Type: None
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: Not Applicable
Average Size: 1.2 to 1.8 meters
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Smooth brownish-grey dorsal color, olive tail with dark-edged scales forming net-like grid. Large eyes.
Active Hours & Habits: Diurnal.
Geographic Distribution in India: Northeast India (Assam, Meghalaya, Arunachal Pradesh) and West Bengal.
Diet & Ecological Role: Frogs, lizards, rodents.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Highly timid and shy; flees rapidly when approached.

[SPECIES: ptyas mucosa]
Common Name: Oriental / Indian Rat Snake
Local Names: Hindi: धामन (Dhaman); Gujarati: ધામણ (Dhaman)
Toxicity & Category: NON-VENOMOUS (Harmless to humans)
Venom Type: None
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: Not Applicable (Non-venomous)
Average Size: 1.5 to 2.4 meters (large, slender)
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Olive-green, yellow-brown, or dark grey body with black vertical bars on the lip scales (labials) and black cross-markings on the hinder part of body and tail.
Active Hours & Habits: Strictly Diurnal; extremely fast runner and agile climber.
Geographic Distribution in India: Pan-India in almost every habitat (villages, agricultural fields, city parks, roofs, and grain stores).
Diet & Ecological Role: Rodents, frogs, birds, and small mammals. Crucial natural pest control agent for farmers.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Non-aggressive. Prefers to flee at high speed. If cornered, inflates its neck vertically, growls, and delivers a quick non-venomous warning bite.
Common Look-alikes: Often mistaken for an unhooded Indian Cobra (Naja naja).

[SPECIES: trimeresurus albolabris]
Common Name: White-lipped Pit Viper
Local Names: Hindi: हरा वाइपर (Hara Viper); Gujarati: લીલો ઝેરી સાપ (Lilo Zeri Sap)
Toxicity & Category: HIGHLY VENOMOUS
Venom Type: Haemotoxic & Pro-coagulant
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: POORLY NEUTRALIZED by standard Indian Polyvalent ASV (Pit viper envenomings require specialized supportive clinical management)
Average Size: 0.6 to 0.9 meters
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Triangular head, heat-sensing loreal pit between eye and nostril, bright green body, distinct white or pale yellow stripe along lower lip (labials).
Active Hours & Habits: Nocturnal and Arboreal (bamboo thickets, low forest bushes).
Geographic Distribution in India: Northeast India (Assam, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh, West Bengal).
Diet & Ecological Role: Birds, small rodents, tree frogs.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Holds its ground on branches, coils in a tight strike posture, and lunges quickly if hands or face approach closely.
Key Clinical Bite Symptoms: Intense local pain, severe swelling of the bitten limb, localized tissue bruising, impaired blood clotting.

[SPECIES: trimeresurus purpureomaculatus]
Common Name: Mangrove Pit Viper / Shore Pit Viper
Local Names: Hindi: मैंग्रोव पिट वाइपर; Gujarati: મેન્ગ્રોવ વાઇપર
Toxicity & Category: HIGHLY VENOMOUS
Venom Type: Haemotoxic & Necrotizing
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: POORLY NEUTRALIZED by standard Indian Polyvalent ASV
Average Size: 0.7 to 1.0 meters
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Deep purplish-brown, dark olive, or black mottled color, heavily keeled scales, triangular head with heat-sensing facial pits.
Active Hours & Habits: Nocturnal; inhabitant of tidal mangrove ecosystems and coastal wetlands.
Geographic Distribution in India: Coastal regions of West Bengal (Sundarbans), Andaman & Nicobar Islands.
Diet & Ecological Role: Fish, crabs, birds, rodents in estuarine habitats.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Highly irritable and quick to strike when disturbed in mangrove branches.
Key Clinical Bite Symptoms: Severe localized edema, intense burning pain, local tissue necrosis, coagulopathy.

[SPECIES: xenochrophis piscator]
Common Name: Checkered Keelback
Local Names: Hindi: ढोंढिया (Dhondhiya); Gujarati: ડંડીયુ (Dandiyu), જળ સાપ (Jal Sap)
Toxicity & Category: NON-VENOMOUS (Harmless to humans)
Venom Type: None
Polyvalent ASV Neutralization: Not Applicable
Average Size: 0.8 to 1.2 meters
Identifiable Traits & Scalation: Olive-green or yellow-brown body with a distinct black checkered net pattern; two black streaks extending backwards from eye. Heavily keeled scales.
Active Hours & Habits: Diurnal and Nocturnal; semi-aquatic (lakes, rivers, drains, paddy fields).
Geographic Distribution in India: Pan-India (found near virtually all freshwater sources).
Diet & Ecological Role: Fish, tadpoles, frogs.
Defensive Behavior & Aggression Level: Feisty and easily agitated when cornered; flattens neck, lunges, and delivers repeated non-venomous bites, but possesses zero venom.
